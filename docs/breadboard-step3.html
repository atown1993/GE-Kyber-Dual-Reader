Step 3 — two RDM6300 readers (TDM) + two common-anode RGB LEDs on the HW-724 ESP32

Reader A = TOP coil (SVP/GPIO36), reader B = BOTTOM coil (SVN/GPIO39). Each reader's GND is switched by a 2N7000 so only one is powered at a time (TDM) — bench-verified working 2026-09-03 with coils ~25 mm apart. Sketch: arduino/kyber_dual/kyber_dual.ino. Wire colors: red = +5V, orange = 3V3, grey = GND, yellow = RDM TX, green = divider tap → RX GPIO, blue = MOSFET gate, purple = antenna, pink = LED legs.

Run these first / notes

  • Rails: TOP pair = 5V / GND (readers). BOTTOM pair = 3V3 / GND (LED anodes). Do NOT put 5V on the bottom + rail — LED anodes must be 3V3. If your board splits rails mid-length, bridge them.
  • Reader GND does NOT go to the rail. It goes to its 2N7000 drain; the MOSFET's source goes to GND. Gate high = reader on. This is the whole TDM trick.
  • 2N7000 pinout, flat face toward you, legs left→right: S · G · D.
  • RGB LED pinout (common anode, 4 legs): longest leg = anode. The single leg on one side of it is R; the two on the other side are G then B. Check with a meter in diode mode if unsure — getting R/G/B swapped just scrambles colors, it doesn't hurt anything.
  • Resistors bridge the trench in the LED columns: one leg in row e, the other in row f of the same column. 220Ω on R, 47Ω on G and B (100Ω was too dim — green/blue dies drop ~3 V, leaving almost nothing across the resistor on a 3.3 V rail).
  • Both dividers are mandatory (ESP32 GPIOs are not 5V tolerant). 10k from TX, 20k to GND, tap between = 3.33V.
  • Coils: scrape + tin the enamel. Mount them facing each other ~40 mm apart so the crystal stands between them — that's the geometry the end-on test proved.
  • Pins are the HW-724's real pinout (photo-verified 2026-09-03, see docs/esp32-hw724-pins.html). Only 13 GPIOs are usable on this board; 0 and 12 are boot pins, safe for MOSFET gates but NOT for LEDs.
  • Reader B's TX/RX/GND/+5V order is the same as A's (TX·RX·NC·GND·+5V, no silk — meter it).
